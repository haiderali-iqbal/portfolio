Undergraduate Thesis Cornerstone

Undergraduate Project Showcase

First-Class Project Mark BSc Biomedical Science (2:1 Overall) Supervisor: Dr. Richard Bingham

Biological Sex Differences and the Limits of Human Endurance: A Bioinformatics Approach to Ultramarathon Data

Abstract & Overview

This research investigated the physiological and statistical limits of human endurance through a sex-comparative lens. By building a scalable data-processing pipeline in R, a global dataset of 7.4 million ultramarathon finishing times was cleaned, standardised, and integrated with population health benchmarks from the NHANES database. The study evaluated how sex-specific differences in substrate utilisation, fat oxidation rates, CPT1-mediated fatty-acid transport, and glycogen sparing influence fatigue kinetics during prolonged exertion, synthesizing computational findings with physiological literature surrounding muscle fibre composition, thermoregulation, and the Central Governor Theory.

Methodology & Analytical Pipeline

Data Processing & Engineering

Built a scalable data-processing pipeline in R and RStudio using tidyverse and dplyr to clean, standardise, and merge massive-scale performance metrics (7.4M records) with external population health datasets (NHANES).

Statistical Analysis

Applied Principal Component Analysis (PCA), Welch's t-tests, and advanced statistical models using rstatix to evaluate multi-variable performance trends and systemic lipid profiles.

Data Visualisation

Generated clean, publication-ready visualisations and exploratory data plots using ggplot2 to clearly illustrate performance gaps across expansion distances and durations.

Physiological Synthesis

Interrogated computational outputs through a metabolic lens—examining lipid oxidation, CPT1 kinetics, neuromuscular fatigue resistance, and the Central Governor Theory.

Statistical Pipeline & Physiological Synthesis

1. Data Ingestion & Quality Control

Ingested raw global race results comprising 7.4 million finishing records. Implemented rigorous data sanitisation protocols in R to remove outliers, validate demographic attributes, and align performance timestamps with external NHANES population health baselines.

2. Principal Component Analysis & Hypothesis Testing

Utilised rstatix and base stats to execute Welch's t-tests and PCA. Demonstrated distinct variance clustering across ultra-endurance distance brackets and biological sex cohorts.

3. Biochemical Interpretation

Synthesised computational outputs with peer-reviewed physiological literature—evaluating how CPT1-mediated fatty-acid transport capacity, glycogen sparing efficiencies, and slow-twitch muscle fibre composition modulate fatigue kinetics during multi-hour exertion.
